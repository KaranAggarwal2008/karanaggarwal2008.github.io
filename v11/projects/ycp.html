IIM RANCHI · 2025

Young Changemakers Programme

A rejection, a return, and Project Sweet Chilli in rural Jharkhand.

The context

I applied to YCP 3.0, was not selected for the programme, and tried again. YCP 4.0 took me to Jharkhand for village immersion.

My role

I was a Young Changemaker on Team Drishti; previously I had been recognised as an Outreach Champion for YCP 3.0.

What we did

In Lohajimi, we investigated elephant-related crop damage and proposed Project Sweet Chilli. I took part in community observation, discussions and the presentation.

The result

Team Drishti finished among the top three of 17 teams at YCP 4.0.

Team & collaborators

Gargi Patankar, Avya Khurana, Prakash Kumar, Vibhuti Patil, Karmishtha Dabral; mentor Dev Tiwari

In my words

YCP made the difference between designing a solution on slides and actually seeing how people experience a problem very clear to me.

Coming back after an unsuccessful interview

I had been involved in YCP outreach and progressed through earlier selection stages, but my YCP 3.0 interview didn't lead to a programme place. I applied again. YCP 4.0 eventually brought me to IIM Ranchi as part of a selected group of roughly 100 participants from several thousand applicants.

The return felt different from getting in on the first try. I had a clearer sense of why I wanted to be there, and I was more willing to learn without needing to prove I already had the answers.

A village, not a classroom case

Our five-day programme included a rural immersion in Lohajimi village, in the Torpa–Khunti region of Jharkhand. The problem we explored concerned elephant movement, crop damage, income uncertainty and human safety. A tidy intervention on paper could create practical problems for farmers if it ignored their schedules, resources or ability to maintain it.

Our work on Project Sweet Chilli developed through that field context. I came away paying more attention to who would implement an idea, how benefits and costs would be distributed, and which questions only the community could answer.

Team Drishti

We finished among the top three teams out of 17 at the bootcamp. That result belonged to the collective effort of the whole group and our mentor.

What stayed with me

Some experiences reinforce what you already believe; this one made my thinking less comfortable. It is possible to be deeply enthusiastic about a proposed solution while not yet understanding the problem well enough. I also learned what a team can achieve when different viewpoints are taken seriously rather than treated as delays.
