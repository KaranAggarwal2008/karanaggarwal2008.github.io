Do lower starting bids lead to higher final auction prices?

Examine whether lower starting bids change final prices and how other auction characteristics matter.

In my words

Collaborative economics research on eBay starting prices, seller reputation and bidding competition.

Original article ↗
The question was narrower than the sales advice

We wanted to test an appealing auction intuition: a low starting price might attract more bidders and drive up the final price. Our study asked whether completed auction data supported that proposition, and whether seller reputation or bidder competition modified the relationship.

How we built the evidence

We combined 628 completed historical listings, representing 136 Cartier items, 343 Palm Pilot listings and 149 Xbox listings, with 200 newly collected concluded eBay auctions. Of the new observations, 187 were usable for the seller-reputation analysis.

Our regression specifications included ordinary least squares, logarithmic forms and HC3 heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors. We examined the main starting-price relationship and interactions with reputation and bidder competition. We also tested robustness rather than relying on a single preferred specification.

What the data said

The results did not validate a claim that lower starting bids systematically produced higher final prices. Instead, the opening bid was positively associated with the closing price in the analysed data. The reported seller-reputation interaction (approximately β = 247.69, p = .848) and bidder-competition interaction (approximately β = 12.56, p = .149) were not statistically significant at conventional levels.

The data are observational and drawn from different contexts, so the findings are evidence about associations and the limits of a common narrative—not proof that a particular causal strategy would work for every seller.
