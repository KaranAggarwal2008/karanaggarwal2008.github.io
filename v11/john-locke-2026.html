WRITING / ESSAYS

John Locke Institute, 2026.

Eight essays across different disciplines, and the questions I tried to answer.

GLOBAL ESSAY PRIZE · 2026

Eight categories. Eight questions.

I received commendations in Economics, International Relations, Public Policy, Theology, Law, Philosophy, Psychology and Science & Technology. I approached them as arguments to be tested, not as research publications.

08
MY ESSAYS AND THE QUESTIONS BEHIND THEM2026
01
ECONOMICS

Beyond the Price Tag: Algorithmic Prediction and the Shifting Grounds of Market Power

I argued that the long-run significance of personalised pricing lies in how valuable predictions about individual consumers become. The consequences depend on competition, information asymmetry and the structure of each market.

02
INTERNATIONAL RELATIONS

The Best Foreign Aid Is Aid That Makes Itself Unnecessary

I explored aid through a long-run institutional question: does assistance create the conditions for independence, or does it make continued assistance necessary? I was interested in when foreign aid genuinely strengthens local capacity.

03
PUBLIC POLICY

When Measures Become Targets: The Unintended Transformation of Education

I argued that when credentials, grades and prestige become the targets of education, students and institutions can end up optimising the signals rather than the underlying learning. Measuring success can change the very behaviour being measured.

04
THEOLOGY

If You Achieve Enlightenment, How Will You Know?

I asked what would count as knowing that someone has achieved enlightenment, and whether conviction, experience or external criteria can reliably establish that claim.

05
LAW

Is Trial by Jury Obsolete?

I considered whether the democratic value of participation in jury trials can be reconciled with the demands of complex evidence and consistent decision-making.

06
PHILOSOPHY

Is It Ever Wrong to Do the Right Thing for the Wrong Reasons?

My central distinction was between an action being beneficial and a person being morally admirable. Outcomes matter for consequences, while intentions also matter when judging character and responsibility.

07
PSYCHOLOGY

Is Mental Illness Over-Diagnosed Now, or Just Better Recognised?

I examined the competing explanations behind rising diagnoses: better recognition and access to care, changes in diagnostic practice, and the risk of treating different experiences as the same condition.

08
SCIENCE & TECHNOLOGY

Should We Be Polite to ChatGPT?

I explored the question of politeness toward AI: what our behaviour toward a system without human feelings might mean for our own habits, relationships and expectations.

These are concise reflections on the questions and arguments behind my entries; the full essay texts are separate documents. The commendations were for the competition entries, not peer-reviewed journal publications.
