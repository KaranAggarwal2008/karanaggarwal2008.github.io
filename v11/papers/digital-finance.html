Digital Finance and SME Financial Inclusion: Evidence, Mechanisms, and Future Directions

Conducted during high school

AUTHORS

Karan Aggarwal (sole author)

STATUS

Manuscript · Submitted

RESEARCH ORIGIN

Conducted during high school

SUMMARY

Systematic literature review of digital finance and SME financial inclusion, with pathways and evidence limitations.

BEHIND THE WORK

A review project that maps mechanisms rather than claiming new causal data.

EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

Access to a digital product is not the same as financial inclusion.

What the review asks

Digital payments, lending systems and other financial technologies are often presented as straightforward solutions for small-business financing. But access to an app does not itself guarantee affordable credit, reliable information or long-term resilience for a small firm.

Mechanisms of inclusion

The literature points to multiple channels: lower transaction costs, more portable financial records, alternative ways of assessing creditworthiness and faster access to services. Each mechanism also depends on connectivity, trust, consumer protection and the financial capabilities of the firms involved.

What kind of paper this is

This project is a systematic literature review intended to organise the evidence, identify recurring pathways and describe limitations in the available studies. It does not claim a new field experiment or an original national estimate of digital-finance impact.

Questions for further research

I'm particularly interested in whether reported adoption leads to meaningful changes in financing constraints and whether digital systems help differently sized or differently located businesses. Those differences are central to evaluating inclusion, not an afterthought.
