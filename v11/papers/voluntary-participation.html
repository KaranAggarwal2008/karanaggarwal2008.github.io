Why Does Voluntary Participation Distort Educational Outcomes? An Institutional Analysis of School-Based Decision-Making Programs in India

Conducted during high school

AUTHORS

Karan Aggarwal (sole author)

STATUS

Published · Peer Reviewed

RESEARCH ORIGIN

Conducted during high school

SUMMARY

Institutional analysis of voluntary participation, selection effects and the interpretation of school-based decision-making programmes.

BEHIND THE WORK

The paper asks whether voluntary participation can distort the apparent success of educational interventions by changing who participates.

Journal article DOI ↗
INSTITUTIONAL ANALYSIS

When joining is a choice, the results can mislead.

The puzzle

Voluntary participation sounds unambiguously positive: people decide for themselves whether to take part. But the people who opt into an educational programme can differ from those who do not, in motivation, support, baseline knowledge or opportunity. Those differences may affect outcomes even before any intervention begins.

Selection and the comparison problem

If participants score better or report higher satisfaction, it isn't automatically clear how much came from the programme and how much from the reasons they enrolled. Institutional rules around access, signalling and participation can therefore change the apparent success of school-based initiatives.

What the paper contributes

The paper examines this problem through an institutional and conceptual analysis, focusing on the interpretation of programme results and the assumptions behind apparently successful interventions. It asks readers to distinguish participation effects from programme effects.

Publication

This sole-author study was published as an article with a registered DOI. Its argument is about understanding the structure of evaluation rather than promoting a universal causal estimate.
