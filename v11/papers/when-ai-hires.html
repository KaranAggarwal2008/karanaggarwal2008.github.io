When AI Hires: A Comparative Analysis of AI Governance Using Resume Audit Evidence

Started during high school · Continued subsequently

Karan Aggarwal (first & corresponding author) · Ridhika Singh

STATUS

Manuscript · In Development

RESEARCH ORIGIN

Started during high school · Continued subsequently

SUMMARY

Compares AI hiring and governance approaches using résumé-audit evidence; not presented as published.

BEHIND THE WORK

Research combining algorithmic hiring questions and comparative AI regulation in India, the EU and United States.

AI GOVERNANCE & EMPLOYMENT

The rules behind a résumé score.

Why hiring is a revealing test

Automated screening systems promise consistency and efficiency, yet hiring is also a setting where a seemingly neutral rule can create different consequences for applicants. A résumé contains signals of education, experience and identity that may interact with how a model was trained or deployed.

The comparative question

The manuscript examines AI hiring governance through the institutional approaches of India, the European Union and the United States. Rather than treating a single algorithm as the whole problem, it considers how regulation, oversight and responsibility differ when the same class of technology is used across legal systems.

The evidence frame

Résumé-audit evidence provides a way to ask how hiring systems respond when candidate characteristics or résumé signals vary. That literature makes it important to separate a claim about discrimination from a claim about a particular country's regulatory design. The manuscript brings those questions together.

Authorship and status

This is collaborative work by Karan Aggarwal and Ridhika Singh. I serve as first and corresponding author. The study is an evolving manuscript; its substantive argument is about hiring technology, institutions and applicant protection rather than a completed commercial product.
