Competitive Incentives and the Persistence of Short-Term Value Destruction in Firm Strategy

Conducted during high school

AUTHORS

Karan Aggarwal (sole author)

STATUS

Published · Pre-Print

RESEARCH ORIGIN

Conducted during high school

SUMMARY

Explores how competitive incentives can reward short-run firm decisions that undermine longer-run value.

BEHIND THE WORK

A research-driven case analysis built from observations about business incentives and publicly discussed firm behaviour.

SSRN DOI ↗
AN INCENTIVE PROBLEM

When winning now can destroy value later.

The basic tension

Companies can reward managers for visible, short-run success while imposing longer-run costs on shareholders, workers or future decision makers. The paper explores how competitive incentives may reinforce decisions that look successful in the current period but erode value over time.

Why firms can keep repeating it

Short reporting horizons, public comparisons and managerial career incentives can favour actions whose immediate results are easy to measure. A firm may therefore be pulled toward a strategy that would be less attractive if all downstream effects were weighed in the same decision.

Method and contribution

This is a research-driven case and conceptual analysis of incentives and publicly discussed firm behaviour, not a controlled test of a single policy. Its contribution is to clarify the mechanisms by which competitive pressure and measurement can shift decisions away from long-term value creation.

Public working version

I made the work available as an SSRN preprint for readers and researchers. A preprint allows discussion and citation before a journal process is complete.
