RESEARCH / CONFERENCE ACCEPTED

Geopolitical shocks & aviation stocks.

I studied how short-duration geopolitical events affected aviation stocks in India.

ConferenceAccepted
WFBS 2026 · RIGA

Impact of Short-Duration Geopolitical Events on Indian Stock Market Returns: Evidence from the Aviation Sector

Conducted during high school · Accepted for presentation in September 2026

AUTHORS — FULL CREDIT

Karan Aggarwal — first & corresponding author
Dr. Anamika Singh
Vishal Kumar

RESEARCH QUESTION

Do short-lived geopolitical shocks affect aviation equities differently from the broader market?

The study examines the Uri attack (2016) and Operation Sindoor (2025), with InterGlobe Aviation (IndiGo) as the exposed company and the BSE Sensex as the market benchmark.

METHOD & INTERPRETATION

A difference-in-differences approach.

The analysis compares relative market movements surrounding the two events. It reports a positive relative movement around Uri and a negative movement around Operation Sindoor, while noting that conclusions depend on identification choices and market dynamics.

THE STUDY BEYOND THE ABSTRACT

What the market actually did.

Why aviation?

Geopolitical shocks can travel through expectations before they travel through company balance sheets. Airlines are exposed to changes in perceived safety, mobility, fuel costs and travel plans. We examined whether market reactions around the Uri attack in September 2016 and Operation Sindoor in 2025 translated into movements for InterGlobe Aviation relative to a broader market benchmark.

Design and comparison

The analysis uses market-price observations, event windows and difference-in-differences comparisons, with the BSE Sensex as a benchmark. For the Uri episode we considered 18 September and the 29 September announcement as distinct possible information dates rather than collapsing them into one event. Alternative windows test how sensitive the estimated movement is to that decision.

Interpreting the pattern

The report finds differing relative movements across the two events, with the interpretation depending on which market window and comparison is chosen. Some exploratory firm-level results show initial declines followed by partial rebounds. That is precisely why identifying a single long-term “shock effect” from a brief news cycle is difficult.

What makes the claim careful

These are observational market data, not randomly assigned shocks. Stock prices move in response to many pieces of information, and the validity of a difference-in-differences interpretation depends on the counterfactual comparison. The goal is to evaluate patterns in specific periods, not to claim that a security event mechanically determines every aviation return.

Research team and guidance

I was the first and corresponding author. Dr Anamika Singh and Vishal Kumar were co-authors and research collaborators, with the wider project developed through a mentored research setting. Responsibility for the paper should be credited to its authors, and the World Finance & Banking Symposium acceptance is a conference milestone rather than journal publication.

A broader question

The project made me more interested in how financial markets process incomplete political information. When a headline looks dramatic, the economist's next question is often less dramatic but more useful: relative to what benchmark, over which window, and with what competing explanations?
