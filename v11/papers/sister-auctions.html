Do Low Starting Bids Trigger Higher Final Prices? An Online-Auction Study

Started during high school · Continued subsequently

AUTHORS

Karan Aggarwal · Aidana Seitova · Tanishk Garg · Advay Panyala

STATUS

Collaborative Study · In Development

RESEARCH ORIGIN

Started during high school · Continued subsequently

SUMMARY

Collaborative analysis of starting bids, seller reputation and bidder competition in eBay-style auctions.

BEHIND THE WORK

Research developed through Synthica SISTER and YEL Economics track; regression and robustness work continue.

Original article ↗
HOW WE TESTED THE HYPOTHESIS

When a compelling idea meets actual bids.

The intuitive claim

Some auction advice suggests starting with a very low price to draw an initial crowd and encourage competition. We asked whether completed eBay auctions supported a higher eventual sale price from that strategy, and whether bidder competition or seller reputation changed the relationship.

A historical sample and new observations

We used 628 historical auction observations—136 Cartier, 343 Palm Pilot and 149 Xbox listings—and collected 200 additional completed eBay auctions. After applying the reputation-data criteria, 187 of the newer cases remained usable. The samples represent different product mixes, so their relationships cannot automatically be treated as identical.

What went into the model

We estimated ordinary least squares models with HC3 robust standard errors, explored logarithmic specifications and tested interactions for bidder competition and seller reputation. This meant looking beyond a simple correlation between the first visible price and the last one.

What did not appear

The main opening-price association was positive in the analysed observations, rather than supporting a general premium from setting very low starting bids. The seller-reputation interaction was reported at approximately β = 247.69, p = .848; the bidder-competition interaction at approximately β = 12.56, p = .149. Neither provided conventionally significant statistical support for the proposed moderation.

A study with four authors

The manuscript credits Karan Aggarwal, Tanishk Garg, Aidana Seitova and Advay Panyala, with equal contributions noted. Tanishk explored bidder competition, Aidana seller reputation and the group developed the broader analysis. A separate IARCO individual proposal addressed a related auction question but should not be conflated with this paper.
