SKILLFLOW — Endogenous Milestones and Vocational-Finance Contracts

SUMMARY

Developing a theoretical model of vocational learning finance, incentive design and endogenous milestones. The manuscript has undergone revision and journal submission attempts; no acceptance is claimed.

BEHIND THE WORK

An ongoing research effort using formal incentive and contract modelling to examine how vocational training might be financed.

THE MODEL AND ITS REVISIONS

Designing incentives without assuming away the learner.

The financing tension

Vocational education takes time and resources, while learners, training providers and financiers don't necessarily benefit from the same milestones. A contract that rewards enrolment rather than skill acquisition can encourage the wrong behaviour; a contract with rigid deadlines can penalise people facing more serious constraints.

Why milestones became endogenous

The project evolved toward modelling how performance or completion milestones could themselves be chosen within a contractual setting, instead of treated as fixed dates handed down from outside. This changes how incentive compatibility, risk sharing and participation are considered.

Revising after rejection

Earlier journal submissions exposed issues around theoretical novelty, model foundations and empirical grounding. I returned to the assumptions, revised the contract structure and worked on a reproducible analysis script that tests sensitivity to key parameters. The point was not to rewrite the abstract until the paper looked more impressive, but to make the underlying mechanism clearer.

What the model can establish

A theoretical model and numerical sensitivity checks can describe incentives under stated assumptions. They don't show how a real training programme performed in practice. The work remains a revised research manuscript rather than an evaluated field intervention.

Why I keep revising it

SKILLFLOW has been a longer exercise in learning to absorb editorial criticism. It made me more interested in the process of making a research claim precise, not only in its eventual publication venue.
